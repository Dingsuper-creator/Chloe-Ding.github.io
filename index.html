Clinical AIReliability

Evaluation and Governance Across Evolving Evidence

My research asks how clinical AI can turn accumulating evidence into an updated patient assessment and a timely, appropriate next action.

From Trustworthy Answers to Safe Clinical Action

One question, three stages

Reliable clinical behavior has to be taught, measured, and tested over time. These projects follow that progression.

Ground judgment in clinician review

Clinician verification and revision turn medical reasoning, safety, and ethics into supervision that a model can learn from.

Test the next action

The central question is whether new information changes what the model believes and what it does next.

Conversation governance

WARD Submitted to ICLR 2027
In longitudinal records, an agent can repeat a test result correctly yet fail to update the patient state, or keep calling tools without advancing the case.

Where behavior breaks

Recognizing evidence is only part of the task. Use the two views to see what can go wrong after evidence arrives.

Escalation arrives too late

Once a risk signal appears, another empathetic question may be the wrong next step. The dialogue study examines when to ask more, revise a response, or recommend escalation.

This work uses a SafeMed-R1 controller and evaluation criteria derived from Med-GRADE.

New evidence A pathology result enters the record.

State update The agent repeats the result but does not revise its assessment.

Next action Tool calls continue without clear case progress.

The record changes; the plan does not

WARD evaluates clinical agents across longitudinal records and tool use. It helps locate evidence omissions, invalid repetition, and failures to progress.

WARD currently diagnoses these failures; it does not establish that the earlier controller solves them.

What comes next

Can patient state guide safer clinical action?

The next comparison is between an unmodified agent, turn-level response review, and state-aware governance. Alongside case progress, it should measure missed evidence, unproductive repetition, premature stopping, extra tool calls, and unnecessary intervention.

The research line is a progression of questions and tests. The five projects are not presented as one already validated end-to-end system.
